About

I design cryptographic protocols for privacy and security. My current focus is end-to-end encryption in the post-quantum setting for the office stack — high-churn chat, video, documents and collaboration tools where the server does the work but never sees the content.

That work draws on threshold cryptography, post-quantum cryptography, and a decade spent making provable security survive contact with real deployments.

I currently lead cryptography research at HOTH in New York. Previously I spent three years at Supra designing and building production cryptographic infrastructure — a rationally secure cross-chain bridge, a distributed VRF with output privacy, and a non-interactive DKG based on class groups. I completed my Ph.D. at Purdue University under Prof. Aniket Kate, on information escrows and efficient key management from threshold cryptography.

Research Interests

  • End-to-end encrypted systems — encryption for documents, mail, storage and collaboration; search, sharing and sync without a readable copy on the server
  • Threshold cryptography — distributed key generation, threshold signatures, secret sharing, information escrows
  • Blockchain security — cross-chain bridges, verifiable randomness, wallet recovery
  • Crypto-economics — mechanism design and game-theoretic analysis of rational adversaries

Applied Cryptography · End-to-End Encryption · Threshold Systems

Easwar Vivek Mangipudi

I design cryptographic protocols for privacy — systems that can hold your data without ever being able to read it. My current focus is end-to-end encryption for the everyday office stack: documents, mail and collaboration tools where the server does the work but never sees the content.

I lead cryptography research at HOTH in New York. Over three years at Supra I designed and shipped HyperLoop, a crypto-economically secure cross-chain bridge; a distributed VRF whose output stays private until it is used; and a non-interactive DKG built on class groups. I earned my Ph.D. at Purdue University with Prof. Aniket Kate, on information escrows and efficient key management from threshold cryptography.

My work appears at IEEE S&P, ACM CCS, IEEE CSF, PoPETs and ACM AFT.

Research

Five threads

End-to-end encrypted systems

Encryption for the everyday office stack — documents, mail, storage and collaboration — where the server can search, share and sync without ever holding a readable copy.

Threshold cryptography

Distributed key generation, threshold signatures, verifiable secret sharing, and information escrows that deter collusion instead of assuming it away.

Post-quantum cryptography

Lattice-based constructions, and the practical problem of migrating deployed systems to post-quantum assumptions.

Blockchain security

Cross-chain bridges, verifiable randomness services, wallet recovery mechanisms, and low-latency anonymity for organizations.

Crypto-economics

Mechanism design and game-theoretic security analysis — arranging incentives so the honest strategy is also the profitable one.
